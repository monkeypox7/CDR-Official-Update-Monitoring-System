News | 12/10/2026 New data shows record number of engineering graduates to strengthen Australia's future workforce The latest higher education data from the Australian Council of Engineering Deans has revealed Australia produced a record 24,253 engineering graduates in 2024, strengthening the skilled workforce needed to deliver on key national ambitions, The report shows 12,206 domestic engineering students completed their qualifications in 2024, a 7.5 per cent increase on the previous year, with international students accounting for a further 12,047 engineering graduates. Among domestic graduates, 8,268 completed bachelor's degrees, while more than 4,100 completed postgraduate or other engineering qualifications. Engineers Australia Chief Engineer Katherine Richards AM CSC FTSE said the results were a step in the right direction to bolstering Australia's engineering talent pipeline. "It is encouraging to see growing numbers of students completing engineering qualifications and strengthening the workforce at a time when engineering skills are needed more than ever," Mrs Richards said. "These engineers will be essential in delivering the projects and innovations that will shape Australia's future, from critical infrastructure to renewable energy, defence capability, and digital technologies." However, Mrs Richards said the nation still needed more graduates to pursue engineering as a career and that women remain underrepresented. "While it is promising to see the number of women choosing engineering slowly climbing, Australia continues to lag behind many of its international peers," she said. "Women make up just three and a half per cent of domestic higher education enrolments in engineering, well below the OECD average of 6.5 per cent. Closing that gap represents one of the biggest opportunities to strengthen Australia's future engineering workforce." The ACED data showed women accounted for 21.7 per cent of domestic students commencing engineering bachelor's degrees in 2024 and 19.5 per cent of domestic bachelor graduates, both record highs. This continues a decade-long upwards trend, with women in 2014 representing just 15.1 per cent of bachelor commencements and 15.3 per cent of bachelor completions. Engineering graduates continue to post strong employment outcomes, with the report showing nearly nine in ten engineering undergraduates were employed full-time and earning a median salary of $82,500 six months after completing their studies. The graduate results also showed 23,599 domestic students commenced engineering studies in 2024, a 10.2 per cent increase on the previous year.
